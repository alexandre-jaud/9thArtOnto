Arc

Description An Arc is a major narrative division of a Story, grouping a set of Events united by a shared narrative thread, dramatic tension, or thematic continuity. Unlike an editorial chapter (a physical division of the book), an Arc is defined purely by narrative coherence. An Arc is composed of one or more Events via hasNarrativeUnit.

Balloon

Description A Balloon is a graphic area containing the text that transcribes speech, thoughts, or narration. It serves as a visual interface between the text and the speaking character, to whom it is generally connected by a tail. Furthermore, the shape of the bubble and the style of its outline (smooth, wavy, jagged) convey important narrative information about the emotional state or tone of voice of the speaker.

Character

Description A Character is the local representation of a MetaCharacter within a panel or a plate. It constitutes the visual and contextual instantiation of that MetaCharacter at a specific moment in the narrative, embedded within a particular spatial and temporal frame. A single MetaCharacter may thus be represented by multiple Characters across different panels or plates, each potentially varying in appearance, posture, expression, or scale depending on the artistic choices of the author.

Event

Description An Event is a NarrativeConstituent that unifies a set of Scenes around a single dramatic question — a causal or intentional thread that persists across changes of location and protagonist cast. An Event opens with an unresolved tension and closes when that tension is resolved or abandoned. Grounded in the causal and intentional dimensions of the Event-Indexing Model (Zwaan & Radvansky 1998) and event segmentation theory (Zacks & Tversky 2001). Corresponds to the macro-event level of Chen (2025). An Event is composed of one or more Scenes via hasNarrativeUnit.

Level

Description A Level is the finest-grained content unit within a Panel. It represents the visual and narrative content of the panel as perceived within a single, coherent time window (T). In the standard case, a panel contains exactly one Level, which groups all the characters, objects, balloons, onomatopoeias, and free text present in the scene at that moment. When a panel depicts several distinct temporal moments simultaneously — a polychronic panel (isPolychronic = true) — it is decomposed into multiple Levels, each corresponding to a different time window: for instance, a foreground showing the present action and a background depicting a simultaneous or past event.

MetaCharacter

Description A MetaCharacter is a persistent actor of the story, identified across all its occurrences in the work. It is not necessarily human and can take many forms, such as an animal, a robot, or an object. Drawn with complete artistic freedom, it very often exhibits significant deformations or disproportions that distance it from real-world proportions. To distinguish it from a background element, its active narrative function is the primary criterion: a true MetaCharacter has a direct influence on the story, which is generally manifested by uttering at least one speech bubble.

NarrativeConstituent

Description Abstract superclass of all narrative units in SequArtOnto. A NarrativeConstituent represents a coherent grouping of narrative content at one of five levels of abstraction, forming a strict tree: Story → Arc → Event → Scene → Segment. The hierarchy integrates three theoretical frameworks: (1) the editorial/structural level (Story, Arc) groups events by narrative coherence rather than by physical book division; (2) the cognitive level (Event, Scene) is grounded in the Event-Indexing Model (Zwaan & Radvansky 1998) and event segmentation theory (Zacks & Tversky 2001); (3) the visual-grammatical level (Segment) corresponds to the VNG constituent (Arc) of Cohn (2013), the only level that directly realises Levels via instantiates and bears a narrativeRole (Establisher, Initial, Peak, Release, Refiner). Location (hasNarrativeLocation) is anchored at the Scene level, as a Scene is defined by spatial, character, and action continuity. Non-leaf levels (Story, Arc, Event, Scene) are realised indirectly through their descendant Segments. Composition is navigated uniformly via hasNarrativeUnit at every level of the hierarchy. Inspired by Cohn (2013, 2020), GOLEM's G9_Narrative_Unit (Pianzola et al. 2025), and Chen's (2025) hierarchical event framework.
URI /seqartonto#NarrativeConstituent
Visual node polygon #6A0DAD stroke #000000
Attributes
NameValuesDescription
narrativeEvent string A free-text description of the narrative event (story-world action, state, or transition) represented by this constituent. Captures the semantic content of what happens at this level of the narrative arc, independently of how it is distributed across panels. Inspired by GOLEM's G5_Narrative_Event (Meghini et al. 2021) and Chen's (2025) event-based hierarchical framework. A leaf constituent may describe a single action (e.g. "The detective picks up the clue"), while a higher-level constituent describes the macro-event it subsumes (e.g. "The detective discovers the truth").

NarrativeLocation

Description A NarrativeLocation represents the diegetic place where narrative events occur. It is the story-world concept of a location, independent of how it is visually rendered in any specific panel. NarrativeLocations form a recursive containment hierarchy via hasSubLocation (e.g. "Paris" contains "Café de Flore"). A single NarrativeLocation instance can be referenced by multiple leaf NarrativeConstituents, providing identity persistence across panels without requiring a separate MetaLocation class. Inspired by GOLEM's G13_Narrative_Location (Pianzola et al. 2025).

Scene

Description A Scene is a NarrativeConstituent defined by the simultaneous stability of two perceptually detectable dimensions: spatial (same NarrativeLocation) and character (same set of protagonists). Following the Event-Indexing Model (Zwaan & Radvansky 1998; Zwaan, Magliano & Graesser 1995), a new Scene begins whenever one of these dimensions shifts significantly — a change of location or a significant change in the protagonist cast. The causal and intentional dimensions of the EIM are delegated to the Event level, which groups Scenes around a shared dramatic question. The Scene bears the hasNarrativeLocation link and is composed of one or more Segments via hasNarrativeUnit.

Segment

Description A Segment is the leaf NarrativeConstituent in the narrative hierarchy. It corresponds to a VNG constituent (Arc) in the sense of Cohn (2013): a grouping of one or more Levels that together bear a single narrative role (Establisher, Initial, Peak, Release, or Refiner) relative to the parent Scene. The Segment is the only level that directly instantiates Levels (via instantiates) and the only level that carries a narrativeRole value. Higher levels (Scene, Event, Arc, Story) are realised indirectly through their descendant Segments. APPLICABILITY WARNING — narrativeRole should only be annotated when the visual track carries the narrative autonomously. As Cohn (2015) cautions: "not all sequences of images use the principles of VNG. In particular, narrative sequences used in multimodal contexts (i.e., combining text and image) may not have a clear-cut narrative structure, due to the meaning being distributed into both text and image. […] The structures of VNG appear most strongly in wordless visual narratives." A practical diagnostic: if deleting the text causes the visuals to lose their overall gist, narrativeRole should be left unassigned on this Segment.
URI /seqartonto#Segment
Visual node polygon #C76BFF stroke #000000
Subclass ofNarrativeConstituent
Attributes
NameValuesDescription
narrativeRole EstablisherInitialPeakRefinerRelease The narrative role of this NarrativeConstituent within its parent arc, following Cohn's Visual Narrative Grammar (2020). Functional: each constituent bears exactly one role. The value is constrained to the following enumeration: "Establisher" (E) — sets the scene without advancing the action; "Initial" (I) — initiates the narrative tension; "Peak" (P) — the climactic moment that heads the constituent and determines its role at the level above; "Release" (R) — resolves the tension after a Peak; "Refiner" (r) — elaborates or modifies an adjacent element without independent narrative force. The role is relative to the constituent's position within its parent: a sub-constituent with role "Peak" is what makes the parent itself a "Peak" at the next level up (recursive heading).

The default case — consecutive constituents in both reading and story order — requires no TemporalLink.

TextSpan

Description A TextSpan represents a word or group of words within a TextLine that share a uniform typographic style. This level of granularity is justified by the fact that typographic style can vary within a single line — a word rendered in bold, italic, or all-caps carries its own meaning and may signal emphasis, vocal stress, or a change of register. Multiple TextSpans within a TextLine are only warranted when there is actual stylistic variation; a stylistically homogeneous line maps to a single TextSpan.

timexText string The original text of the temporal expression as it appears in the caption (e.g. "Three years later", "The next morning").
timexType DATEDURATIONSETTIME The type of temporal expression, following TimeML's TIMEX3 typology (ISO 24617-1): DATE (calendar date), TIME (time of day), DURATION (elapsed time, e.g. "three years"), SET (recurring expressions, e.g. "every morning").
timexValue string The normalized ISO 8601 value of the temporal expression (e.g. "P3Y" for "three years", "1944" for a year reference). Optional when normalization is not possible.

⟶ hasLocative

Description Links an Interaction to the Object that serves as its spatial reference point. Corresponds to the locative thematic role (Fillmore 1968): the object at, near, or in relation to which the action or spatial relation takes place (e.g. a wall, a table, a door). ⟶ instantiates

Description Links a Segment (leaf NarrativeConstituent) to the Level(s) of the comic that visually realise it. Only Segments carry instantiates relations directly: higher-level constituents (Scene, Event, Arc, Story) are realised indirectly through the instantiates of their descendant Segments.
